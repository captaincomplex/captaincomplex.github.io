Minimum Fuel

Both fuel calls protect one number: the final reserve on your flight plan. What you compare it with is the fuel you'll land with, not the fuel you have now.

Hold, divert or commit

You're holding near the destination. Run the clock and watch the lines on the gauge pass the fuel you have.

The holding scenario needs JavaScript. In short: you can divert while you'd land at the alternate with final reserve. Once you can't, you're committed to the destination: call MINIMUM FUEL. If you'd then land there below final reserve, call MAYDAY FUEL.

Illustrative A320 figures: holding burn 38 kg/min, 200 kg from the hold to the runway, planned final reserve 1,100 kg. When diverting, "close to final reserve" is taken as within 300 kg; the rules give no number. Use your flight plan, FMS and operations manual for real figures.

Two kinds of diversion fuel

  • Planned alternate fuelOn the flight plan: missed approach at the destination, climb, cruise, and approach at the alternate.
  • Diversion fuel from the holdFrom where you are, already at holding level. Often less than planned: no missed approach, no climb from low level. More if the hold is on the far side, away from the alternate.
  • So there are two limitsStarting the approach with less than planned alternate fuel plus final reserve makes it one-shot: after a go-around you can't reach the alternate. Diverting straight from the hold can stay possible for longer, or end sooner.

MINIMUM FUEL, MAYDAY FUEL, or neither?

  1. 1

    Holding. Diverting now: land at the alternate with final reserve + 400 kg. Waiting: land at destination with final reserve + 700 kg.

    Answer

    Neither. Two options, both protect final reserve.

  2. 2

    Ten minutes later. Diverting now would land you below final reserve. Waiting still lands you at destination with final reserve + 300 kg.

    Answer

    MINIMUM FUEL. The alternate has gone, so you're committed. Any change could put you below final reserve.

  3. 3

    After MINIMUM FUEL, ATC adds 12 minutes. You'd now land at destination, the nearest suitable aerodrome, 150 kg below final reserve.

    Answer

    MAYDAY FUEL. Predicted landing fuel is below planned final reserve.

  4. 4

    Gauges: 1,500 kg. Planned final reserve: 1,100 kg. Nearest suitable aerodrome: 15 minutes at 40 kg/min.

    Answer

    MAYDAY FUEL. You'd land with about 900 kg. The gauges don't matter; predicted landing fuel does.

  5. 5

    Runway blocked: go-around and divert. Cleared direct to the alternate, landing with final reserve + 100 kg.

    Answer

    MINIMUM FUEL. Committed to the alternate and close to final reserve.

  6. 6

    You called MINIMUM FUEL. ATC: "Roger, expect 10 minutes." After that you'd still land with final reserve + 500 kg.

    Answer

    Neither: no new call. MINIMUM FUEL gets no priority, so the delay is normal. Keep recalculating.

More detail and references

The fuel on your flight plan
  • Taxi, trip: to take-off, then to landing at destination.
  • Contingency: 5% of trip fuel, at least 5 minutes' holding (basic scheme; approved variations differ).
  • Destination alternate: missed approach at destination to landing at the alternate. With no alternate, 15 minutes' holding instead.
  • Final reserve: 30 minutes' holding at 1,500 ft above the aerodrome, at the expected landing mass.
  • Additional, extra, discretionary: for specific failures or routes, expected delays, and the commander's choice.
