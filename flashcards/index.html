Normal law protections

B – Bank angle protection
S – Speed protection
L – Load factor protection
A – Angle of attack protection (alpha prot)

What is alternate law?

A minimum of two or more failures must occur for the flight controls to degrade from normal law.

Examples:
Multiple flight control computer failures
Multiple hydraulic system failures
Multiple ADIRU failures

Explain bank angle protection in normal law.

Max roll rate 15°/s
Positive spiral static stability up to 33°
Max bank angle 67°
During AoA protection, max bank 45° (still returns to 33° when released)
During high speed protection, max bank 40° (returning to 0° when released)

If bank exceeds 45° the AP disconnects and the FDs disappear (reappearing at 40°).

Which protection has authority over all others?

High AoA protection

In alpha prot, what happens when you release the sidestick?

The alpha prot value is maintained.

Can high speed protection be overridden?

Yes, at first, but nose-down authority is progressively reduced to zero.

What protections do we have in direct law?

None.

Explain unusual attitude law.

Gives the PF maximum efficiency to recover a normal attitude.

Engages if:
Bank angle >125°
Pitch +50°/−30° (if not 2 ADCs valid and consistent: +40°/−20°)
Speed <70–90 kt / >440 kt / >M.91
AoA >40°

Pitch alternate law with no load factor limitation, roll direct law, yaw mechanical, "USE MAN PITCH TRIM".

On recovery to the normal flight envelope: F/CTL ALTN LAW.

What is the load alleviation function?

Loads on the wing are reduced by the ailerons deflecting up and the spoilers deploying. (Normal law only.)

Why should "USE MAN PITCH TRIM" be ignored with a G+Y hydraulic failure?

The THS hydraulic motors are powered by G and Y, so you can't trim once in direct law. CONF 3 and VAPP must be achieved before gear down.

Which failure gives you direct law without pitch trim? ("USE MAN PITCH TRIM" still displayed)

HYD G+Y fail

A320 only (with AP, half to full deflection is the same)

A. LOCK (alpha/speed lock) function

Inhibits slat retraction at high AoA and low speed.

When is the beta target displayed?

All of:
CONF 1–3 selected
Any N1 >80% (or one at or above MCT)
N1s differ by >35%

On the ground, the sideslip index shows lateral acceleration of the aircraft (against its stop at 0.3 g).

In flight it shows sideslip as computed by the FAC. When centred, sideslip equals the sideslip target for best performance.

Sidestick priority: what does the red arrow mean?

That side has lost authority (the arrow points towards the pilot in control).

Sidestick priority: what does the green CAPT/F/O light mean?

That side has taken control and the other sidestick is not neutral (CAPT light = CM1 has taken over from CM2).

The light goes out when the other sidestick returns to neutral.

When is the sidestick indication shown on the PFD? When does it disappear?

On the ground, after first engine start.

It disappears when the aircraft goes from ground to flight.

Flaps/slats jammed: catches?

AP disconnects by 500 ft AGL, so CAT 1 only.

Speeds and guidance 14

VS1g

Stall speed at 1 g load factor.

The A320 has low-speed protection that cannot be overridden. This allows a smaller margin, as stall speeds for conventional aircraft are based on a load factor below 1 g.
VSmin = 0.94 VS1g

V2 = 1.2 × 0.94 = 1.13 VS1g
Vref = 1.3 × 0.94 = 1.23 VS1g

VLS

Lowest selectable speed.

Clean: 1.28 VS
Retraction of one stage of flaps: 1.23 VS
T/O: 1.13 VS

Above 20,000 ft: corrected for Mach effect to keep a 0.2 g buffet margin.

Computed by the FAC, based on aerodynamic data.

How is VLS calculated?

Computed by the FAC from FMS weight data, with aerodynamic data as a backup.

1.13 VS (T/O)
1.23 VS (after retracting one stage of flaps)
1.28 VS (CONF 0)

Corrected above 20,000 ft to keep a 0.2 g buffet margin.

OEI operating speed in CONF 0.
Below 20,000 ft: 2 × weight (t) + 85
Above 20,000 ft: add 1 kt per 1,000 ft

2 × weight (t) + 85

Above 20,000 ft: add 1 kt per 1,000 ft

1.23 VS (CONF FULL)

How is V2 determined?

Minimum 1.13 VS.

Takeoff safety speed reached by 35 ft with one engine inoperative, and maintained during the second segment.

Why are there differences between speeds on the PFD and MCDU?Varies by type and MSN

PFD: computed by the FAC, from FMS weight data (A321/A320neo), aerodynamic data (A319, some A320s), or both.

MCDU: computed by the FMS, from gross weight based on entered ZFW + FOB, or predicted GW.

Explain SRS.

Speed reference system. Uses pitch to hold a target speed.

Takeoff: V2 + 10 kt
OEI: the higher of V2 or current speed, up to V2 + 15 kt
Go-around: current speed at go-around initiation (max VLS + 25 kt, min VAPP). OEI go-around: max VLS + 15 kt.

Attitude protection limits nose-up on takeoff (max 18°; 22.5° in windshear).

FPA protection ensures at least 120 ft/min climb.

Explain RWY mode.

Holds the runway LOC path. Needs SRS (so V2), a LOC signal, and to be within 20°.

Reverts to NAV at around 30 ft.

What powers the APU?

DC BAT BUS

What is the APU flap for?

Protects the APU from ingesting FOD, fluid etc.

Why keep the batteries on while shutting down the APU (with no external power)?

Below 95% the APU can't supply electrical power. As it shuts down, power is needed from somewhere to close the APU flap. If left open, anything could get into the inlet.

APU model in use since 2017 on A320s?

Honeywell 131-9A

What is fuel inerting?

A system that reduces flammability in the centre fuel tank.

It produces oxygen-depleted air from bleed air and replaces the ambient air in the centre tank with air containing less than 12% oxygen.

What is the fuel feed sequence on the A319/A320?

1. Centre tank
2. Inner tanks (down to 750 kg each)
3. Outer tanks (transfer to inner tanks)

What is the fuel feed sequence on the A321?

1. Centre tank
2. Wing tanks

What is the refuelling sequence?

If no centre tank fuel: outer wing tank, then via the spill valve to the inner tank.

If centre tank fuel: outer wing tank and centre tank at the same time until full, then inner tank.

What is the fuel burn sequence?

From the centre outwards.

Centre tank, inner wing tanks until 750 kg remain, then outer wing tanks feed the inner tanks.

Slat logic: during takeoff, to avoid the centre tank feeding both engines, the centre tank pumps are off until the slats are retracted.

With jet pumps, 500 kg is used from the inner tanks first so that IDG-heated fuel has somewhere to go, preventing spillage.

Why is the outer wing tank temperature sometimes higher than the inner?

The IDG heat exchanger: returned fuel heats the outer tank.

Benefits of centre tank jet pumps?

Pressure from the inner tank fuel pumps creates a venturi effect: as inner tank fuel is pumped through the jet pump, it sucks fuel out of the centre tank, all feeding the inner tank. A simple design that saves weight, cost and maintenance.

Engines are only ever fed from their own side; the jet pumps act as transfer pumps to the inner tank.

What drives the accessory gearbox?

The HP (N2) shaft.

Bleed air sources?

HP compressor stages.

CFM: 5 (IP, engine anti-ice), 9 (HP, hydraulics)
Pneumatics: the 5th stage is normally enough, but at idle or in descent the 9th may be added.
Hydraulics: bleed air at 50 psi to the reservoirs, preventing pump cavitation.
LPTCC: fan air. HPTCC: 5 + 9. RACC: fan + 5, blown onto HP compressor stage 3.

LEAP: 4 (IP), 7 (engine anti-ice, starter bleed, booster anti-ice), 10 (HP, hydraulics)
LPTCC: fan air. HPTCC: fan air. Modulated turbine cooling: 10. Transient bleed valve: 10.

What is HPTCC?

High pressure turbine clearance control.

Uses bleed air from stages 5 + 9 to cool the HP turbine casing, minimising the gap between casing and blades created by thermal expansion.

Also LPTCC.

(LEAP: taken from fan air.)

What is RACC?

Rotor active clearance control.

Air blown onto the rotor blades in the HP compressor.

Modulated turbine cooling?

Air blown onto the HP turbine blades. Like RACC but for the turbine. (LEAP only.)

What does the transient bleed valve do?

Bleeds off pressure at a certain compressor stage to prevent engine surge.

LEAP only.

Booster anti-ice?

Anti-ice for the LP compressor, using air from the HP compressor.

How would you know booster anti-ice is active on the LEAP-1A?

"CORE" appears on the ENG SD page. Very rare.

Why does the LEAP take longer to start than the CFM?

Thermal stabilisation. After shutdown, hot air rises and the top of the spool expands, bowing the rotor. With quick turnarounds this causes vibration and wear. The LEAP dry cranks so hot and cold air mix and the spool straightens out.

Engine start sequences?

CFM:
IGN: pack flow control valves close
Master on: LP fuel shutoff valve opens; starter valve opens (APU bleed air to the accessory gearbox starts turning N2)
16% N2: igniter A/B on
22% N2: HP fuel shutoff valve opens
50% N2: start valve closes, igniter off (self-sustaining)
30 s after: pack flow control valves open

LEAP:
IGN: pack flow control valves close
Master on: LP fuel valve opens; starter valve opens
15%+ N2: igniter A/B on
20%+ N2: HP fuel shutoff valve opens

Different types of idle thrust?

Modulated idle: on the ground, or in flight CONF 0 (NEO: gear up, CONF 0–2). Regulated according to bleed demand.

Approach idle: in flight CONF 1–FULL (NEO: gear down, CONF 3/FULL). Regulated according to altitude, so the engine can accelerate quickly.

Reverse idle: REV IDLE selected. Slightly higher than forward idle.

Is anti-skid available on the alternate brakes?

Yes, provided the A/SKID & N/W STRG switch is not off and there is no power failure, BSCU failure or G+Y HYD fault.

Abnormals 16

Summarise each dual hydraulic failure.

G+B: handling problem. Gear down at 200 kt.
G+Y: stopping problem. The worst one.
B+Y: the easiest one. Normal law.

When can we call "engine secure"?

No damage: ENG MASTER off
Damage: one agent discharged
Fire: both agents discharged, or fire extinguished

What does each turn of the gravity gear extension crank do?

Isolates the L/G hydraulics from the G system.

Unlocks the L/G doors and gear.

Allows gravity to drop the gear into the extended position.

When does the RAT extend automatically?

AC BUS 1 + 2 fault above 100 kt.

It supplies pressure to the hydraulic motor that drives the emergency generator.

AC ESS and DC ESS (via the ESS TR) are supplied by the emergency generator.

When will the RAT extend automatically? How long does it take?

AC BUS 1 + 2 fail and the ADIRS detects speed above 100 kt.

Takes about 8 s for the RAT to extend and the EMER GEN to couple.

What is available in ELEC EMER CONFIG?

See QRH: ELEC EMER CONFIG systems remaining.

CM1 displays and the EWD.

VHF 1, VOR 1, ELAC 1, FMGC 1, etc.

Engine fire in ELEC EMER CONFIG: what might you need to consider?

The agents are powered by different sources: agent 1 by the BAT 1 + 2 HOT BUS, agent 2 by DC 2. So in ELEC EMER CONFIG you only have one agent per engine.

DC ESS BUS fault: catches?

AP disconnects, but AP 2 is available. B HYD lost (pump); pressure indications for all hydraulic systems are lost (a green triangle still shows >1450 psi).

RMP 1 is blank. AUDIO SWITCHING to F/O 3 and turning up the CM2 speaker is the only way both pilots can hear the radio.

STATUS says CAT 3A, but with A/THR inop only CAT 2 can be flown.

HP fuel valves are inop, so the engines are shut down with the FIRE pb (closes the LP fuel valves). Shutdown can take about 1 min due to residual fuel in the line.

With SDAC 1 + 2 fault, which parameters are still available?

L/G, fuel, engine, F/CTL

(LGCIU, FQI, FADEC and FCDC bypass the SDACs.)

During an engine failure, can the APU bleed be used?

If the ENG 1 FIRE pb is pushed, APU bleed use is prohibited (APU bleed feeds the ENG 1 side of the system).

Do not use APU bleed for wing anti-ice.

GEN 1 LINE pb?

Lets the crew dedicate GEN 1 to powering only one fuel pump in each wing. Used in the avionics smoke procedure.

CAB PR EXCESS ALT: actions?

Below FL160: apply the ECAM procedure; initiate a normal descent
Above FL160: apply the EMER DESCENT memory items; apply the ECAM procedure

One reason we cannot dispatch without the CIDS?

Smoke detection in the lavatories and cargo hold goes via the CIDS to the FWC.

You've pushed back and released the ground crew, about to get taxi clearance. An ECAM appears. Do you consult the MEL?

Yes. Consequences for the next flight? Implications for takeoff/departure? If it says no dispatch, would you still go? Any actions to complete? Call/ACARS maintenance control?

Suspected bird strike close to V1: actions and considerations?

Go-minded. Advise ATC.

Check engine indications. Ask the cabin crew about any strange odours from the air conditioning. If a bird strike is suspected with no indications, continue and contact maintenance control. If it has caused suspected damage, consider a return.

How to decide whether to declare PAN or MAYDAY?

As a general guide, if the successful outcome of the situation is in jeopardy, a distress call (MAYDAY) is appropriate.

A distress call also puts the surrounding emergency services on standby.

It can be downgraded or cancelled at the crew's discretion.

What does NAV ACCURACY HIGH/LOW mean?

EPU does not exceed RNP: HIGH.
EPU exceeds RNP: LOW.

What is RLD?

Required landing distance = ALD × regulatory factor.

Dry runway: ALD dry × 1.67
Wet runway: ALD dry × 1.92
Contaminated runway: ALD contaminated × 1.15

What is a precautionary landing?

An anticipated landing decided on by the crew to manage an abnormal situation (e.g. technical failure, illness), but not an emergency.

Under normal circumstances an evacuation will not be necessary.

After landing, cabin crew stay seated and await instructions: "Normal operations" / "Senior cabin crew to the flight deck" / "Evacuate, unfasten your seatbelts and get out".
