Five logo options for xpdr.aero

“XPDR” is the standard abbreviation for a transponder, the box that answers a radar’s question with “this is me”. Every option plays on that. Each is shown as a mark on its own, as a lockup with the name, on dark and on light, and at favicon size (32 and 16 pixels).

01

Squawk ring

The closest cousin of the mark already on the site: a ring with a dot at its heart, the way a radar draws a target, with four tick marks like a compass or a dial. Calm and geometric.

02

Squawk box

A squawk code is what a pilot dials into the transponder: four digits, each in its own box. Here the four boxes hold X, P, D and R. A crisp, typographic option that reads as “XPDR” at a glance.

03

Sweep

A radar screen: two range rings, a sweeping beam, and one bright blip that has just answered. It says “aviation” more loudly than the others, and the blip gives it a focal point. It has the finest detail, so it will suffer most at 16 pixels.

04

Pulse X

A bold “X” made of two crossing strokes, one in the blue-to-violet gradient and one in the text colour, with a small reply dot where they meet. The most graphic of the five; being two simple strokes, it should also survive being shrunk to a favicon better than the finer ones.

On the light background the reply dot keeps its dark fill from the dark page; the final version would swap it to match.

05

Reply

An app-icon style tile holding a small aircraft with two arcs of signal above it: the transponder answering. It is the friendliest and the most literal, and it is the easiest to recognise as an icon on a phone home screen.

These are first sketches, drawn as scalable vector graphics so they can be resized without going soft. Pick one (or mix: say “the ring with the sweep beam”) and it can be refined and exported as a favicon and app icon.
