Five strategies, same situation

How the model works: at idle, clean, the aircraft descends about 3 NM per 1,000 ft (a little steeper when faster) and slows about 10 kt per NM in level flight. In DES, managed speed may vary ±20 kt around its target, capped at VMAX (350 kt / M.82), and no more than 5 kt above a 250 kt target below FL100; below the path DES rejoins at about 1,000 ft/min. Half speedbrake steepens the path by about 25% at 300 kt, less when slower; with the autopilot engaged the A320 gives no more than half; it is inhibited in CONF FULL, and once the gear is down the spoilers are armed for landing (operator SOP), so no speedbrake. Gear and flaps add drag; VFE 230/200/185/177 kt, VLO 250 kt, VLE 280 kt. EXPED descends at idle at 340 kt / M.80. Passing the DECEL point (D) activates the approach phase, or activate it earlier yourself: managed speed then targets green dot 210 kt, then S 180, F 150, 145 and 140 kt. The descent path allows for slowing to approach speeds before 3,000 ft. The glideslope is 3° and can be captured within 20 NM. Stable at 1,000 ft here means on the glideslope, gear down, CONF 3 or FULL and below VAPP + 30 (VAPP 140 kt FULL, 145 kt CONF 3), following one operator's criteria; yours may differ. Approach speeds depend on weight and drag figures are approximate; your FCOM and FCTM are the authority.

What people get wrong

“High managed speed in DES is safe: the FMS won't overspeed.”

Above the path, DES stays at idle and lets speed run up to the top of its range. With a high managed speed that range reaches VMAX, so there's no margin left: a gust or a tailwind change gives an overspeed. Select a lower speed, or use the speedbrake before the speed builds. Try “High managed speed” and press Gust.

“DES holds the path at the target speed.”

It holds the path within a speed range, not both exactly. Above the path it adds no drag for you: it asks for MORE DRAG.

“The speedbrake will fix it.”

Its effect falls with speed, and with the autopilot on you get at most half. Gear and flaps add more drag, but only once you've slowed below their limits.

“3 × altitude is enough.”

Add for tailwind and for slowing to 250 kt at FL100. Together that's often 10–15 NM.
