# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Existing codebase: static HTML and CSS served by GitHub Pages at xpdr.aero. No build step, no framework, no scripts required to read it. Fonts are self-hosted.

## Users

- Makers with a Raspberry Pi, who may build Spotipi Photo or Equalize themselves from the open-source code.
- Pilots and crew, who may use the aviation tools (Unable, Divert Diplomat, Spotted) and the Ident flight-duty board.
- Photo and Mac users, who may use Perspective or Format on their own computer.

They arrive to see what has been made and to follow a link to it (a repository, or Ident's own site). Visitors are not expected to sign up, log in or buy on this site.

## Product Purpose

xpdr.aero is the home of one working airline pilot's projects, published under the trading identity xpdr.aero. Its job is to show what has been made and give each project a clear link to where it lives. Success is a visitor finding the project they came for, understanding in a few seconds what it is and whether it is ready, and following the link.

## Positioning

A single maker's own tools: wall displays and small programs that run on the visitor's own hardware (a Raspberry Pi, their own computer) rather than a service. Several are free and open source, and the site says plainly which are working, which are early, and which are not published.

## Operating Context

Projects on the site: Spotipi Photo (64×64 LED panel for photos and album covers; free and open source; built on Ryan Ward's Spotipi), Ident (flight-duty board, a product with its own site at ident.xpdr.aero), Equalize (LED equalizer; ready for release; runs on a real panel; works with Spotipi Photo on one Pi), Perspective (finds well-composed photos in Apple Photos), and the small tools Format, Unable, Spotted, Divert Diplomat and Plant Keeper-Aliver. You're Famous (e-ink picture frame) is unpublished and marked "needs work, to do later". Equalize has its own page at /equalize/.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Each project's status wording must match its repository's own state; do not claim more than a repository says.
- Ident's roster privacy claim belongs to Ident's own site. Do not restate it here beyond what that site says.
- Static hosting only. The site must work without JavaScript.
- Open undecided facts: licences of the small tools are per repository and not confirmed here; Ident pricing is not shown on this site.

## Brand Commitments

- Trading identity is xpdr.aero. The owner's real name stays off anything public.
- The xpdr.aero logo (the dotted beacon with the A2 lockup, plus the black and white backups) is final; the four lockups are the main logos and the dot proportions stay identical everywhere. See `logo/README.md`.
- Each project keeps its own logo or icon where it has one: Spotipi Photo, Equalize (LED ring), Perspective (P icon and the thirds-lines logo), Ident (A + A2 lockup from the Ident repository). Projects without a logo show none; do not invent one.
- Credit to Ryan Ward, creator of Spotipi, stays on the page. Spotipi Photo is built on Spotipi and Equalize on Spotipi Photo.

## Evidence on Hand

- Screens of Spotipi Photo, Equalize and Perspective, and illustrations for the small tools, in `assets/`.
- Equalize's animations are rendered by its own code from a demo track, not recorded from a real panel. Do not present them as recordings.
- No testimonials, customer counts or usage figures exist. Do not invent any.

## Product Principles

1. Show what exists, honestly: status labels say working, early or unpublished as the repositories do.
2. Every project is one click from where it lives.
3. Free and open source is stated plainly where it is true, with credit where credit is due.
4. Quiet over busy: a visitor should be able to take in the whole page without effort.
5. One identity across the site: one type system, the same logo proportions, every project treated alike.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific standard was stated. The working bar is readable text sizes and contrast, keyboard focus on every link, and a layout that holds from phone width up.
